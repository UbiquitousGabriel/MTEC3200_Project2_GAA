# Interview Script

**Problem territory:** how people in a community ask for help with everyday things, and how people willing to help find out who needs it.

**Length:** ~15 minutes · **Format:** in person (public place) or phone/video

## Before starting
1. Explain the purpose: a City Tech class design research project about how people ask for and offer help.
2. Read the consent statement (see `recruiting_script.md`) and ask permission to record. If declined, take written notes.
3. Remind them they can skip any question or stop at any time.
4. Explain their responses are used in a class project without their full name.
5. Write "Consent given" + date at the top of the notes file.

**Interviewer reminders:** ask about past experiences, not hypotheticals · ask about a specific time, not "usually" · allow 3 seconds of silence before continuing · ask "why" even if I think I know · **do not describe what I plan to build.**

---

## Theme 1 — Their community (warm-up, most factual)
1. Tell me a little about the neighborhood or community you're part of.
2. Who are the people around you that you see or talk to in a typical week?

## Theme 2 — Needing help
3. Tell me about the last time you needed help with something you couldn't easily do alone.
4. Who did you ask, and how did you reach them?
5. Walk me through what happened after you asked.
6. Tell me about a time you needed help but didn't ask. What stopped you?

## Theme 3 — Offering help
7. Tell me about the last time you helped someone outside your family or close friends.
8. How did you find out they needed help?
9. Tell me about a time you would have helped someone, but didn't know until it was too late (or never found out).

## Theme 4 — Trust and follow-through (most personal)
10. Tell me about a time asking for help, or helping someone, felt uncomfortable or went wrong. What happened?
11. When someone you don't know well asks you for help, what makes you say yes or no? Tell me about the last time that happened.
12. What do you currently use to keep track of people who need help, or people you can count on? Show me if you can.

## Closing
13. Is there anything else you think I should know?
14. Is there anyone else you think I should speak with?
15. May I contact you later in the semester for feedback? (Note their preferred contact method in the private key, not in GitHub.)

---

### Question-writing notes
- Brainstormed freely, then grouped into 4 themes.
- Removed yes/no and hypothetical questions ("Would you use…?" / "If there were a way to…?"), rewriting them as "Tell me about the last time you…".
- Ordered from most factual (community) to most personal (trust, discomfort).
